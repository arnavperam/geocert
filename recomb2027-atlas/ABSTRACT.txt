Certified Expression Intervals under Shared Segmentation Uncertainty
Arnav Peram

Imaging-based spatial transcriptomics assigns each detected molecule to a cell, so a shared boundary error can change several expression counts together. We compute the smallest and largest retained cell-by-gene counts attainable under a declared physical deformation budget. For ordered boundaries with slope, band, thickness and nuclear constraints, a polynomial-size rational cut represents the minimum continuous deformation of every feasible assignment vector. The evaluated single-interface case admits a canonical nearest realization and an exact critical-height mesh. A count-indexed dynamic program computes its complete hard-budget spectrum in polynomial time for explicitly listed point transcripts, including outcomes missed by every penalty bound. Disjoint boundary ribbons glue into a topology-preserving deformation of the reference partition; min-plus composition allocates one area budget across their spectra. Fixed rules yield 544 ribbons on lung crops and 1,708 on external breast format-test crops, with 210,240 exact interval rows. Among original cell–gene questions where at least two transcripts can move, shared geometry tightens 4 of 46 lung and 203 of 1,026 breast questions. A prespecified physical-width sensitivity study permits displacement caps up to 2.23 micrometres and quantifies the loss of coverage as wider ribbons are required. Comparisons with nuclear expansion and Proseg executions expose assignment changes outside every tested family. The resulting certificates are globally consistent, inspectable sensitivity statements for observed counts under fixed seams, with anatomical calibration remaining a separate empirical requirement.
