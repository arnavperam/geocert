GeoCert: Certified Expression Intervals under Segmentation Uncertainty in Spatial Transcriptomics
Arnav Peram

A shared cell-boundary error can alter several expression counts together in imaging-based spatial transcriptomics. We introduce GeoCert, an exact method for determining which observed cell-by-gene counts remain invariant under a physical boundary-deformation budget and which can change. For ordered boundaries subject to slope, band, thickness and nuclear constraints, one polynomial-size graph with rational capacities represents the minimum continuous deformation of every feasible molecular assignment. In the single-interface point-transcript setting, an exact critical-height construction yields a polynomial-time count dynamic program that computes hard-budget optima even when the best penalty bound is loose. Disjoint boundary ribbons combine into one topology-preserving tissue partition, and min-plus composition allocates a shared area budget across them. A finite union of complete atlases admits alternative ribbon widths while retaining every primary feasible boundary. On Xenium lung crops and a breast format-test dataset, five atlas families per crop yield 496,604 exact interval rows over 17 budgets. At 4 µm², their union widens 298 lung and 3,595 breast intervals relative to the primary atlas. Across the budget grid, shared geometry tightens independent-molecule outer bounds for 29 of 102 lung and 791 of 2,076 breast questions whose outer width reaches at least two. The same effect appears in two breast cancer donors: under the primary atlas, shared geometry tightens independent-molecule bounds for 13.8% and 11.7% of eligible cell–gene pairs. Attaining boundaries certify each interval endpoint and reveal when several molecular transfers share one deformation. GeoCert turns a specified segmentation uncertainty model into exact expression-count intervals and inspectable geometric witnesses.
