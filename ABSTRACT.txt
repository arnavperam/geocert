Title: Exact Joint Deformation Costs for Continuous RNA-Ownership Sensitivity

Cell boundaries determine the gene-count profiles measured by imaging-based spatial transcriptomics. Several transcript reassignments can share one boundary displacement, so adding their individual deformation costs can misrepresent the sensitivity of those profiles. We study the minimum continuous deformation realizing each joint assignment in an ordered local cell corridor. For heterogeneous slope limits, thickness bounds, boundary bands, fixed endpoints and nuclear regions, we prove that propagated envelopes admit a simultaneously nearest boundary. Exact integration gives a polynomial-size rational graph preserving this entire assignment-cost function before unary molecular costs are specified. Direct elimination aggregates recurring activation sets without retaining boundary-height choices; an exact critical-mesh construction provides a second representation. Fixed reassignment requests admit a simpler closure algorithm when the source realizes all source-owner constraints. On 42 selected corridors from one Xenium donor, a same-feasibility additive ablation changes the optimum in 1 of 126 baseline and 16 of 126 relaxed-family questions. In the baseline example, a shared displacement of approximately 3.506102 µm² transfers five transcripts, switching four genes from detected only in one neighboring cell to detected only in the other. The decision persists over penalties 0.67–1.28 RNA/µm². The method supplies exact local gene-count sensitivity witnesses under explicit, uncalibrated geometric assumptions.
