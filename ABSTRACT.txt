Certified Expression Intervals under Shared Segmentation Uncertainty
Arnav Peram

A shared cell-boundary error can alter several expression counts together in imaging-based spatial transcriptomics. We introduce GeoCert, an exact method for determining which observed cell-by-gene counts remain invariant under a physical boundary-deformation budget and which can change. For ordered boundaries subject to slope, band, thickness and nuclear constraints, one polynomial-size rational cut represents the minimum continuous deformation of every feasible molecular assignment. In the single-interface point-transcript setting, an exact critical-height construction yields a polynomial-time count dynamic program that recovers outcomes missed by every penalty bound. Disjoint boundary ribbons combine into one topology-preserving tissue partition, and min-plus composition allocates a shared area budget across them. Applied to Xenium lung and breast crops, GeoCert constructs 544 and 1,708 ribbons and computes 210,240 exact interval rows. At a budget of 4 square micrometres, 84.1% of lung and 77.1% of breast cell–gene counts are invariant within the primary atlas. Among questions with at least two individually movable transcripts, shared geometry tightens independent bounds for 4 of 46 lung and 203 of 1,026 breast questions. Attaining boundaries explain the remaining count variation and reveal when several molecular transfers share one deformation. GeoCert turns a specified segmentation uncertainty model into exact expression-count intervals and inspectable geometric witnesses.
