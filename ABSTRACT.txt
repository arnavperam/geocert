Certified Expression Intervals under Shared Segmentation Uncertainty
Arnav Peram

A shared cell-boundary error can alter several expression counts together in imaging-based spatial transcriptomics. We introduce GeoCert, an exact method for determining which observed cell-by-gene counts remain invariant under a physical boundary-deformation budget and which can change. For ordered boundaries subject to slope, band, thickness and nuclear constraints, one polynomial-size rational cut represents the minimum continuous deformation of every feasible molecular assignment. In the single-interface point-transcript setting, an exact critical-height construction yields a polynomial-time count dynamic program that recovers outcomes missed by every penalty bound. Disjoint boundary ribbons combine into one topology-preserving tissue partition, and min-plus composition allocates a shared area budget across them. A finite union of complete atlases admits alternative ribbon widths while retaining every primary feasible boundary. On Xenium lung crops and a breast format-test dataset, five atlas families per crop yield 496,604 exact interval rows over 17 budgets. At 4 µm², their union widens 298 lung and 3,595 breast intervals relative to the primary atlas. Across the budget grid, shared geometry tightens independent-molecule outer bounds for 29 of 102 lung and 791 of 2,076 breast questions whose outer width reaches at least two. Under the primary atlas, a separate 600 µm square yields 1,223,400 exact interval rows over eight budgets for 3,171 complete cells. Attaining boundaries certify each interval endpoint and reveal when several molecular transfers share one deformation. GeoCert turns a specified segmentation uncertainty model into exact expression-count intervals and inspectable geometric witnesses.
