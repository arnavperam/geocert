Title: Exact Joint Deformation Costs for Continuous RNA-Ownership Sensitivity

RNA reassignment decisions share a cell geometry: several changes can use the same displaced boundary, while individually feasible changes may be incompatible. For ordered continuous cell corridors with heterogeneous Lipschitz limits, thickness bounds, bands, fixed endpoints, and structural regions, we prove a polynomial-size rational graph representation of the minimum integrated deformation for every feasible ownership vector. Propagated envelopes admit a simultaneously nearest realization; analytical integration then preserves the complete ownership-cost function before arbitrary unary molecular costs are chosen. An exact critical-mesh height construction preserves the same function, while direct integration eliminates its height choices and aggregates recurring activation costs. If the source realizes every source-owner obligation at zero deformation, pinned queries admit a simpler implication-closure and envelope-reconstruction algorithm. On 42 selected corridors from one Xenium development donor, an additive singleton-cost surrogate with identical feasibility and favorable tie handling is strictly suboptimal in 1 of 126 baseline queries and 16 of 126 relaxed-family queries. A baseline five-versus-one reassignment decision persists over penalties from 0.67 to 1.28 RNA/µm². Across 252 matched optional queries, our direct implementation and exact critical-mesh reference give identical exact objectives; after equal hard-implication contraction, median total times are 4.53 and 19.54 seconds. These are exact, corridor-conditioned sensitivity results under uncalibrated assumptions, not corrected anatomical ownership or whole-tissue validation.
